# TCP echo before and after the `try_io` fast path (porto-szb.148), 2026-11-13

`bench/micro/echo_probe.py --workers 8 --rounds 3000 --reps 21 --only porto,porto-read,uvloop`
(interleaved, release wheel, GIL 3.14, Apple M4 Pro; microseconds per 1 KiB round trip, 100
connections). `porto` is `serve_tcp` with `read_exact(1024)` + `write` (the pyperf row);
`porto-read` is the same with `read(1024)`. **The machine was not quiet** (load average 10 to 12
from other projects' builds), so read the medians as ±5 %, and the "after" run had a burst of
interference in the middle of it (reps 10 to 15).

| server | before median | before min | after median | after min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| porto (`read_exact`) | 6.53 | 6.20 | 6.28 | 5.28 |
| porto-read (`read`) | 6.50 | 6.01 | 6.40 | 5.06 |
| uvloop streams (same session) | 6.69 / 6.65 | 6.23 | 6.65 | 6.02 |

What it shows: the medians moved by 4 % (`read_exact`) and 1.5 % (`read`), inside the noise; the
minimums, the least disturbed samples, moved by about 15 %. The skipped `recvfrom` is real but
small next to the rest of a round trip, and a quiet machine has to confirm the size of it.

Why `read_exact` gains less than it might: the skip needs the stream to know the socket is empty.
A receive that returns less than the room it had proves that; `read_exact(n)` asks for exactly
`n` (the spec, 17 §1.1, says it receives no more than it needs, so the rest stays in the kernel),
and a receive that fills its room proves nothing. So a request/response server that reads
fixed-size frames with `read_exact` still pays the receive that finds the socket empty once per
round trip; one that reads with `read`, `read_buffer`, `readline` or `read_until` does not. Letting
`read_exact` read ahead would remove it, at the price of that contract (a follow-up decision).
